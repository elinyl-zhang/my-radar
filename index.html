02 / Think — What idea is worth carrying?
Cognitive Offloading & Distributed Memory

What happens to memory when your tools remember for you — and what happens when they stop?

A Nature paper examines how digital tools that store, surface, and synthesize information change the relationship between remembering and knowing. The infrastructure of that memory is fragile: platforms sunset, APIs break, formats rot.

This reframes digital preservation from an archival problem to a cognitive one. If your intellectual practice depends on externalized memory, then the durability of that memory is the durability of your thinking.

Your daily radar itself is an act of cognitive offloading. What's your fallback when the radar goes dark?

Nature →
Code as Hermeneutic Practice

Can Python be a humanities method — not just a utility?

When humanists write interfaces, not just scripts, they're making interpretive claims about how knowledge should be encountered. The GUI is an argument. Python becomes a medium through which you think about text.

The distinction between 'using code' and 'thinking in code' is the boundary between digital humanities as service and digital humanities as practice.

03 / Look — What should train your eye?

Studio Dumbar

Dutch graphic design studio, Rotterdam

Studio Dumbar's recent identity for the Rotterdam Philharmonic Orchestra reimagines how motion and classical form coexist — a live case study in designing for institutions that must be both historic and contemporary.

Look for
  • Typographic scale that creates hierarchy without hierarchy — large forms that don't dominate, small forms that don't disappear.
  • The interplay of photographic and geometric elements: where does the image stop and the system begin?
  • Color as structure, not decoration — color fields that divide space the way a grid would, but with more ambiguity.

04 / Discover — Who should enter your world?

Johanna Drucker

Scholar · Critic · Book Artist · Typographer

Breslauer Professor of Bibliographical Studies at UCLA. One of the most incisive thinkers on the materiality of the page — physical and digital. Her work spans artist's books, critical theory, digital humanities, and the history of writing.

Drucker's critique of the 'flat' digital page — the assumption that content is independent of its graphical presentation — is becoming more urgent as AI-generated text floods every surface. If form is content, then AI that outputs plain text is not just incomplete; it's wrong about what text is.
